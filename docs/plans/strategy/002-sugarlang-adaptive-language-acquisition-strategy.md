## North star

A mind-blowing, genuinely fun language-acquisition RPG: Zelda-shaped, but instead of fighting things you acquire Spanish or Italian by living in a world. Sugarlang is the plugin that makes THIS game special. It should feel like the world's most patient bilingual companion is inside every conversation -- speech arrives exactly at the edge of what you can understand, the right concept shows up at the narratively right moment, and the characters never stop being characters.

Fun first is a pedagogical position, not a compromise: the evidence says learning gains ride on the game being a real game (see invariants below). When teaching and a scene fight, the scene wins that turn and the teaching reschedules.

The three install modes stay contractual (Plan 018):
- sugarlang alone: the full teaching loop over authored dialogues and quests, PLAYABLE with zero runtime LLM (the baked floor, epic C); when a gateway is present, scripted lines render adaptively too.
- sugaragent alone: generic agentified NPCs for any sugarmagic game, no teaching.
- both: adaptive free conversation held at the learner's level. The one seam remains the opaque `sugarlang.constraint` annotation; nothing new crosses the boundary.

## What the science says (2026 sweep; condensed, sources at end)

1. Input is the engine, not the whole car. Massive comprehensible input drives most acquisition (Krashen's core survives; his strong claims did not). Interaction -- clarification, rephrasing, recasts -- measurably adds (Long, Gass); output forces grammar processing that comprehension never does (Swain); noticing support (highlighting, glossing) is required for intake (Schmidt). Consequence: scripted reading mode is the acquisition engine; agent mode is the interaction/output engine; both need salience and retrieval scheduling.
2. Functions are chunks. The notional-functional syllabus (Wilkins) -> CEFR can-do descriptors is the settled way to sequence CONCEPTS like greeting and thanking. Formulaic sequences are stored whole and carry most pragmatic appropriateness (Wray): teach "buenos dias" / "grazie mille" as unanalyzed wholes, decompose much later or never. Situational placement alone underteaches -- explicit beats implicit in pragmatics instruction (Taguchi 2015, 58 studies): the situation plus ONE light explicit line is the winning combination.
3. The mixing budget is generous. 98% known-token coverage was the classic comprehension threshold, but the 2023 registered replication (Kremmel) softened it: degradation is gradual, and in a game where every item is hover-glossable and the L1 frame carries the plot, 95-98% is the comfort zone and 90% the frustration floor -- roughly one new item per sentence. Each introduced item needs ~8-10 DIVERSE encounters (different sentences, speakers, scenes) to stick (Webb; Uchihara): re-feeding is as important as introducing. Frequency-first selection: the top 2-3k families give 90-95% of everyday coverage (Nation). Words a quest makes instrumentally NECESSARY retain best (involvement load; Yanagisawa and Webb meta).
4. Four strands audit (Nation): meaning-focused input (NPC reading) and output (typed replies) and deliberate study (glossing, review) all exist or are planned; FLUENCY -- easy recycling of only well-known material at speed -- is the strand games always forget. The teacher's outer loop schedules it.
5. Anti-edutainment invariants are the difference between Wagotabi and shovelware. Documented failure modes: players skim/optimize past the learning (observed even in the best-reviewed title); interactivity itself can crowd out vocabulary (deHaan); extrinsic rewards around drills kill intrinsic motivation. And the affect side is our friend: games reliably lower language anxiety, and errors must never be socially punished -- only pragmatically felt in fiction (Sykes; TESL-EJ game-pragmatics review: visible NPC reactions + cheap retries help; speed-rewarding design hurts).
6. Diglot weave is an on-ramp, not a destination. L2-words-in-L1-frames has positive (weak) direct evidence and is coverage-compliant by construction, but it cannot teach word order, morphology, or connected comprehension. The support-posture ladder must actually graduate learners to full L2 clauses or they plateau at "English with Spanish nouns."
7. Level control is a verification problem, not a prompting problem. Prompt-only CEFR targeting matches the intended level ~5% of the time (EJ1466280) and drifts toward mid-level across turns (arXiv 2505.08351): the envelope classifier must run per-turn forever, in both modes. Best-of-N sampling scored by our own classifier roughly HALVES control error at no quality cost (Duolingo, ACL 2024) -- the cheapest proven upgrade to verify/repair. Hard token-masked decoding requires logit access we do not have through hosted models (watchlist: self-hosted small model). Two-pass write-freely-then-simplify preserves meaning better than constrain-first.
8. Persona under simplification is an OPEN research problem -- no benchmark, no paper. The practical answer comes from games craft: personality lives in level-free channels (signature interjections, punctuation and tempo, gesture/action tags, barks), so constrain the lexical channel and leave the voice channel free, and add our own voice-retention check beside the CEFR check.
9. Learner modeling: no published method feeds read/hover/produce gradations into FSRS -- our observation grade table is AHEAD of the literature; keep it, instrument it, tune from our own data (IFA is the framework ancestor). Grading open-ended typed replies via an LLM labeler that tags (item/function, demonstrated?) is proven (LLMKT, LAK 2025). Quiz-free comprehension estimation from behavior alone is unvalidated everywhere; every working system manufactures cheap graded micro-interactions instead -- so the fiction must ask.
10. Immersion's effectiveness is carried by micro-exchanges. Negotiation of meaning -- comprehension checks, confirmation checks, clarification requests, recasts during meaning-focused talk -- has direct experimental support (Long 1996; Gass and Varonis 1994; Ellis, Tanaka and Yamazaki 1994). Graduated assistance doubles as assessment: the amount of help a learner needs to succeed IS the measurement (dynamic assessment, Lantolf and Poehner; contingent scaffolding, Wood, Bruner and Ross 1976 -- less help after success, more after struggle). Consequence: the NPC behaves like an interlocutor, not an oracle -- probe, notice, repair, check, move on -- with the teacher deciding when and what kind; repair episodes are the best learner evidence the model ever gets.
11. The "god person watching" has a name and it is not an LLM. Intelligent-tutoring outer loop (VanLehn) + learning-progress curriculum bandits (ZPDES) + Duolingo's pattern (predictive model scores candidates, deterministic assembler packs to a target success rate) + Left 4 Dead's AI Director (deterministic intensity curve -> content constraints). The 2025-26 tutoring literature explicitly recommends externalizing pedagogy decisions into an interpretable orchestrator that CONSTRAINS generation, over letting the LLM improvise teaching. Only academic ancestor of curriculum-plus-narrative joint direction: Crystal Island (pre-LLM). The LLM-era combination is unclaimed territory.

## Market position (mid-2026, verified)

All three gaps in our pitch are open. Wagotabi (99% positive, real sales) proves demand for "a game where you learn language, not a gamified program" -- and is fully scripted with a 262-word ceiling. Newcomer, the only shipped LLM-NPC language RPG, sits at 77% with reviews hammering unchecked machine translation and zero level governance. Duolingo Max's Video Call reviews as "a novelty... 30 seconds... too basic." Nobody ships: (a) a fun-first game with level-held LLM dialogue, (b) learner-model-driven curriculum orchestration inside a game world, (c) level-simplified speech that keeps personality. Sharp signals: TRUST in generated target-language accuracy is the number-one review killer for LLM-era titles (bake-time review directly answers this); Steam players favor one-time purchase and punish undisclosed genAI; adaptive diglot density as a per-player dial has never shipped in any game (Toucan proved the mechanic is liked, then died lacking exactly the retention loop we have).

## Settled architecture

### The invariants (anti-edutainment contract)

- Language rides the critical path: quest-critical information travels IN the target-language portion (questEssentialLemmas graduates from an exemption list to an enforced authoring affordance). Skimming must never be the winning strategy.
- Comprehension is checked by the fiction, not by quizzes: reply chips, yes/no beats, an NPC asking for something that requires a due word. These micro-interactions are ALSO the graded evidence stream the learner model needs.
- Errors are never socially punished. NPCs react to pragmatics in fiction (warmth, confusion, delight); retrying a conversation is cheap and normalized; recasts model the correct form inside the NPC's natural reply.
- The encounter contract: introducing an item creates a scheduler DEBT of ~10 diverse re-encounters across NPCs, scenes, and days. Introduction without re-feeding is wasted budget and is tracked as such.
- Fun first: no turn is allowed to feel like a worksheet. The teacher reschedules; it never forces.
- As complicated as it needs to be, no more: every child epic defines its FLOOR (the minimal version that ships value) and its CEILING (the adaptive ideal). Floors ship first; ceilings must earn their complexity with playtest evidence.
- Language progression is DECOUPLED from narrative progression. Any player, at any fluency, starts season 1 episode 1 act 1, and the SAME authored content adapts. Episodes are never constructed around curriculum -- there is no "greetings episode," ever. The teacher figures out how to realize the curriculum inside the scene, cast, script, and quest AS WRITTEN: for a true beginner that means introducing greetings with whoever is present; for an intermediate player the same episode renders at their level and negotiated meaning takes it from there. The learner portrait moves; the story does not.

### Functions are chunks (the curriculum spine)

The curriculum becomes a hand-curated inventory of communicative FUNCTIONS (greet, thank, request, apologize, ask price, ask directions, refuse politely...) keyed to CEFR can-do descriptors, each realized by high-frequency CHUNKS taught as unanalyzed wholes. Chunks are first-class FSRS items beside lemmas -- the types, serialization, and classifier pre-pass already exist; the extractor (internal epic 14's LLM-as-metadata-author at bake time) finally gets built, extended to tag which functions each scene and NPC can realize. Teaching a function = the situation that calls for it (FOUND in content as authored, per the decoupling invariant -- content is never written to serve the syllabus) + one light explicit beat (a journal note or mentor line: "that was a greeting; here is the polite form") + in-fiction pragmatic feedback. Grammar stays out of scope as explicit content; it arrives implicitly through graduated full-L2 clauses and recasts.

### The Teacher, grown up (the god-watcher is the teacher's outer loop)

No new component. The "someone wise watching the whole board who knows the player is about to meet a lot of new people and that this is the moment for greetings" is the TEACHER -- the component that already exists in code (SugarLangTeacher and its policies) -- grown from a per-conversation reactor into the standard two-loop tutor shape (VanLehn). The OUTER LOOP is new and deterministic: it schedules across scenes and days. The INNER LOOP is today's teacher policy, unchanged in seam: it realizes the schedule into PedagogicalDirectives and constraints per conversation, LLM-backed with the deterministic fallback and directive cache. One name, one owner of "what to teach when."

The outer loop:

- READS: the learner model (FSRS due queue, CEFR posterior, encounter debts, a rolling comprehension-strain estimate from hovers, probe failures, and slow replies); curriculum state (functions not yet introduced, prerequisite graph); and the WHOLE BOARD as neutral published facts -- quest graph lookahead, the dialogue tree around the current line, the scene's contents (NPC roster, items, inspectables), player-character facts (a history witch gets the history of the place), blackboard world events (someone just arrived or left, the weather, time-of-day), and sugaragent.memory firstMeeting when sugaragent is present. In sugarlang-alone mode it reads quest, scene, and dialogue-graph state only.
- DECIDES: WHAT to schedule (ZPDES-style -- teach what is currently yielding learning progress inside the prerequisite graph, packed to a target comprehension rate rather than a raw word count), WHEN (L4D-style strain curve -- teach-peaks alternate with consolidation valleys and fluency-recycling scenes that use only well-known items at ease), WHETHER THIS LINE IS THE MOMENT (a scripted line whose intent can carry the due teachable triggers a directed live render; see the rendering ladder), and WHETHER TO CHECK (does this turn warrant a negotiation move -- comprehension check, confirmation, clarification, recast -- and which kind; see Immersion, negotiated). The band+1 ceiling is a STRETCH ALLOWANCE the teacher spends deliberately: when an opportunity is unusually good, it may schedule a slightly-beyond-level teachable, rendered in terms the learner already understands.
- EMITS: scheduled teachables into the inner loop, which turns them into PedagogicalDirectives and constraints; for agent NPCs it may also bias retrieval toward teachable-relevant lore (the 077 quest-aware-retrieve seam, pointed at pedagogy). The teacher never writes narrative and never picks story beats; it only ever constrains language rendering, so it cannot conflict with the agent architecture.

On Strategy 001's "no director" ruling: that ruling was about NARRATIVE -- a central LLM deciding story for agentified NPCs -- and its justifications do not transfer here. The teacher's outer loop stays deterministic on its own merits: the tutoring literature is unambiguous that interpretable orchestration beats LLM-improvised pedagogy, and a deterministic scheduler is explainable, tunable, and free. Where a model IS the right tool -- inner-loop directive phrasing, rendering -- it is used, behind verification.

### Immersion, negotiated (micro-exchanges are the adaptive mechanism)

The experience north star for the teacher: a four-week immersion trip with a private tutor who ARRANGES it. Not a classroom, and not being thrown in alone. The tutor plans the itinerary -- which places you go, which people you meet, so that each day emphasizes a concept -- and has quietly slipped the barista, the cheesemaker, and the old man in the park a note and a tip: speak to this one at such-and-such pace, with simple, somewhat simple, average, or fluent vocabulary. Then the world does the talking, and the tutor steps in with exactly as much help as needed. In our terms: the itinerary is the teacher's outer loop, the tip to the locals is the constraint, and the in-the-moment help is the repair ladder. Real immersion teaches through MICRO-EXCHANGES -- probe, notice, repair, check, move on -- and that loop, not the band system, is the primary adaptive mechanism AND the primary assessment.

- Negotiation of meaning is core NPC behavior, and deciding it is the TEACHER'S job: whether this turn warrants a move, when, and what kind -- comprehension check ("Sabes queso?"), confirmation check, clarification request, recast of a player error (Long 1996; Gass and Varonis 1994; Ellis, Tanaka and Yamazaki 1994 -- the most-supported mechanism in interactionist SLA; the 2026 sweep's design note was explicit that LLM practice should be built on negotiation moves, not free chat). The wire format already exists: the constraint's interactionStyle and comprehensionCheckInFlight fields.
- The repair ladder, in order: rephrase in target language using known words -> deictic grounding (the NPC points at the actual entity; the game's most native teaching mechanic, unavailable to any chat app -- full scene-wired version is a STRETCH GOAL below; the floor is textual/emote reference to the entity by name) -> mime/emote -> support-language gloss as last resort -> check understanding -> move on with life. Graduated assistance doubles as measurement: the amount of help needed IS the assessment (dynamic assessment -- Lantolf and Poehner 2004/2008; contingent scaffolding -- Wood, Bruner and Ross 1976: less help after success, more after struggle, the softest adaptive rule there is).
- Non-understanding has detectable signals: response latency, hover bursts, incoherent replies, explicit "que?" moves out of Interpret. Repair episodes are the HIGHEST-QUALITY learner evidence we get -- better than any injected quiz -- and feed the model as such.
- Bands become priors, not law. CEFR stays as the breadcrumb trail (placement prior, coarse shared impression, bake-time banding); the live exchange calibrates from there. The envelope keeps its teeth as the rendering guardrail, but its target is per-learner and strain-adjusted rather than a fixed cutoff (comprehension degrades gradually, no cliff -- Kremmel et al. 2023; "i+1" was never an operationalized unit).
- The learner model is repositioned, not weakened. The player's brain is the progress bar; the store does only the two jobs the brain cannot do for us. (1) The SHARED IMPRESSION: every NPC gets, via the teacher, the same evolving "they seem about here" first-guess -- the village feels like it knows you, and nobody re-probes from zero. A good first guess is what makes repair occasional and charming instead of constant and exhausting. Its NPC-facing form is a LEARNER PORTRAIT: one carefully authored prose description per band, grounded in the CEFR can-do descriptors but written the way a tutor describes a person ("knows basic nouns and verbs, nothing advanced yet" / "intermediate -- could pick words out of TV in the target language but can't keep up"), plus live per-learner deltas on top (knows the food words; struggled with past tense). Constantly re-probed, never re-asked. This is also the measured fix for level control: rich level descriptions plus exemplars roughly halve control error versus bare level labels (Malik et al. 2024). (2) The RE-FEED SCHEDULER: pure immersion never guarantees the 8-10 diverse re-encounters a word needs; our world quietly arranges them (encounter debts). Assessment is conversational-first; explicit probes are diegetic and teacher-scheduled, never quizzes.
- Curriculum item zero: the META-LANGUAGE chunks that let the player drive negotiation IN the target language -- "Que es...?", "No entiendo", "Mas despacio, por favor", "Como se dice...?" -- gated by placement. The placement fiction already exists (the station manager's faux customs form seeds every NPC's "they are about here, act accordingly"): if the verdict says these moves are known, assume them and never teach them; if the player is a true beginner, the station manager introduces them immediately and the teacher reinforces on a DECAYING reminder lifetime ("Remember, you can always ask: Que es?"). NPCs honor these moves with the repair ladder from the other side.

### Voice survives the envelope (two-channel speech)

NPC speech decomposes into a lexical channel (constrained by the envelope) and a voice channel (level-free): per-NPC signature interjections and exclamations whitelisted as free vocabulary, punctuation and tempo habits, action/gesture tags rendered outside the text envelope, barks later. The character stays loud while the words simplify -- "Why good day to you, my fine sir, what a glorious vista" becomes "Ah! Buenos dias, senor. *sweeps hat* Bonita vista, no?" not "Hello. Nice view." Authoring source per mode: the lore page `## Voice` section when sugaragent is present; NPC definition fields in sugarlang-alone mode.

Verification doubles: beside the envelope verdict, a cheap voice-retention check (similarity against per-NPC exemplar lines, or one rubric line where the judge already runs) so repair cannot flatten the character -- the literature has no joint CEFR+persona benchmark; this two-channel + double-verify design is the engineering answer. The verify loop upgrades from retry-on-fail to best-of-N sample-and-select (proven ~2x control-error reduction, masked by the existing typing indicator). Repair guidance shifts from word-swapping to SAY IT SIMPLER: preserve the message, cap the syntax, keep the voice channel exempt -- simplification of the concept's expression, not substitution of words.

### Lines are intent; rendering is adaptive (one renderer, two intent sources)

A scripted line's ground truth is not its English surface text -- it is the INTENT: the must-convey facts (including questEssentialLemmas-linked info), the dramatic beat, the voice. The authored English is the canonical expression of that intent and the fidelity anchor every rendering is verified against. Scripted and agent mode share ONE rendering principle -- realize this intent, at this learner's level, in this NPC's voice, teaching what the teacher scheduled -- with two intent sources: the authored line (scripted) or the plan stage's response goal (agent). Division of labor is fixed: the TEACHER decides, the model RENDERS, the verifiers CHECK (envelope + must-convey fidelity + voice retention). No model ever decides what to teach or what a scene means; it only phrases and renders.

The rendering ladder for scripted lines:
- Deterministic weave (anchored/supported postures, the early game): the authored English verbatim as the frame, runtime substitution of the live prescription (introduce/reinforce lemmas and chunks, correct surface forms from atlas morphology, glossing per strategy). Full per-learner, per-moment adaptivity ("struggling with purchases -> this line introduces comprar NOW") with zero LLM, zero translation risk, writing voice untouched by construction.
- Baked floor (deep-end postures, full L2 clauses): per-line variants generated at Studio bake across the deep-end bands, content-hash cached, shipped as assets, triple-verified at bake against the authored English. Studio surfaces an exception-review report readable WITHOUT speaking the language (flagged lines, English back-glosses, fidelity scores); the finite corpus is auditable by a native speaker later, which pure runtime generation can never be. The floor guarantees the Strategy 001 contract: authored mode PLAYABLE with zero runtime LLM, offline, always.
- Directed live render (the ceiling, when a gateway is present): when the teacher spots an opportunity the baked variant cannot serve -- the learner's due concept fits HERE; the scene context (who is present, what just happened, who the player is) makes this the teaching moment -- it re-renders the line live from intent + teachable + scene, through the SAME triple verification at runtime; on any verify failure the baked variant plays. Cached by (line, band, posture, teachables): cost scales with teaching opportunities, not with players times lines.

Today's runtime scripted call is still deleted (bias toward deletion): it is unconditional, unverified against intent, uncached, and has no floor. What replaces it is opportunity-triggered, verified, cached, and floored. No general-purpose MT API (Google Translate or otherwise): the job is level-constrained, voice-preserving rendering, not faithful translation, and it runs through the existing gateway.

Agent mode is inherently runtime: there the envelope classifier runs per-turn forever (drift is real and grows with conversation length), with best-of-N, double verification, and constraint re-injection cadence.

Turn budget discipline: the common-path agent turn gets at most ONE model call beyond today's generate + judge. The envelope verdict is deterministic; the voice check rides the judge rubric or the repair prompt, the reply labeler rides interpret or observe, and best-of-N REPLACES repair calls rather than adding to them. A turn needing more than three model calls end-to-end is a design smell, not a feature.

### Evidence channels (the learner model earns its keep)

- Typed replies get graded: an LLMKT-style labeler (one cheap call riding the observe path) tags player utterances with (item or function, demonstrated correctly?) -- produced-* observations stop being string-match-only, and FUNCTIONS become trackable skills, not just words.
- Recasts become a first-class directive: when the player errs, the NPC's reply naturally models the correct form; whether the player adopts the recast is itself evidence.
- Micro-interactions manufacture graded evidence in fiction (reply chips, comprehension beats, retrieval-in-fiction drawn from the FSRS due queue); the probe framework finally gets its UI.
- Passive exposure stays weak-positive signal; large updates gate on production. The observation grade table is ahead of published work: keep it, instrument it (telemetry lands in epic A), and tune the weights from our own data.

### How the two plugins play one scene (lifecycle)

Domain model in one sentence: sugaragent owns the CHARACTER (who is speaking, what they know and remember, what they are trying to say); sugarlang owns the LANGUAGE (at what level it comes out, what it teaches, what the player's response proves they learned). Every interaction is one plugin handing the other a note, never a merged brain. The theater version: sugaragent is the actor; sugarlang is the teacher in the wings. The teacher slips the actor a note before the scene; the actor performs in character; the teacher checks the take and may ask for one more; then the teacher watches the audience and updates the lesson plan. The actor never knows why the note says what it says; the teacher never tells the actor what the scene is about.

Stage by stage, both installed:

- Before any conversation (teacher outer loop): reads the board -- due queue, encounter debts, curriculum graph, quest lookahead, scene contents, blackboard world events, and sugaragent's published annotations (sugaragent.memory firstMeeting, sugaragent.questContext) -- and produces the schedule. Sugaragent is not involved; the outer loop only reads what is already published.
- Conversation open (prepare stage): each plugin's middlewares load their own context side by side (persona/memory/quest vs learner profile/scene lexicon). The teacher's inner loop realizes the schedule into a PedagogicalDirective and writes the one crossing: execution.annotations["sugarlang.constraint"].
- Interpret (sugaragent): player utterance -> intent/socialMove/etc. Sugarlang reads these outputs as LEARNER EVIDENCE (produced a farewell, attempted a due word, asked for clarification, signaled non-understanding -- the blank stare has proxies). Consumes, never modifies.
- Retrieve (sugaragent): teachable-relevant retrieval bias arrives via the constraint (the 077 seam pointed at pedagogy), never by sugarlang reaching in.
- Plan (sugaragent): response intent + goal -- the agent-mode INTENT SOURCE, the equivalent of the authored line in scripted mode.
- Generate (sugaragent): the renderer. Reads the constraint, folds the overlay into the prompt (the existing six-line splice). This is where "realize this intent, at this level, in this voice, teaching what the teacher scheduled" happens.
- Judge (sugaragent) then verify (sugarlang): two verdicts in one turn, each plugin judging only its own domain -- character/world fidelity vs envelope + voice retention. On sugarlang's fail: best-of-N / bounded repair, riding sugaragent's regenerate machinery.
- Observe (sugarlang, last): observations from the final text and player interaction (encountered / hovered / produced) update FSRS; the decorator gets gloss/highlight annotations.
- Conversation close: sugaragent writes NPC memory; sugarlang rolls session signals into the learner profile. Deliberate asymmetry: NPC memory dies on New Game, learner knowledge survives it.

Degradation is just "the note is missing." Sugaragent alone: no constraint annotation, NPCs speak unconstrained -- a generic agentified game. Sugarlang alone: no actor, so sugarlang is the whole show -- intent comes from authored lines, rendering is the weave / baked-floor / live-render ladder, and interpret-derived evidence is replaced by dialogue-choice and probe evidence. Nothing in any mode checks whether the other plugin is installed; each finds the other's artifacts on the bus, or does not.

### Plugin boundary (unchanged, restated)

sugarlang -> sugaragent: only `sugarlang.constraint`. sugaragent -> anyone: annotations (`sugaragent.memory`, `sugaragent.questContext`) and runtimeContext facts. The teacher consumes only neutral published facts and degrades gracefully when sugaragent is absent. ADR 010 boundary tests extend to every new surface. Nothing in this strategy adds a cross-import in either direction.

## How we know it is teaching (verifiable goals)

- Mechanical, from telemetry (epic A makes this real): FSRS predicted-vs-observed recall on re-encounters (is the memory model calibrated?); envelope hold rate per turn in both modes; repair frequency per learner trending DOWN over time (the contingency rule visibly working); posture graduation events (nobody stuck at "English with Spanish nouns").
- Human, from playtest: the can-do function inventory doubles as the test rubric -- a player who finished the episode should be able to greet, ask what something is, and buy the thing, cold, outside the game. Periodic and small; no in-game exams.
- Anti-metrics: time-in-dialogue and words-introduced-per-session are NOT success metrics; both reward the documented failure modes (skimming, introduction without re-feeding).

## Stretch goals (the wired world)

Recorded here deliberately: both need real game wiring beyond the dialogue layer, neither blocks any child epic, and together they define the ceiling of "the world itself is the classroom."

- Full deictic grounding: the NPC points at, looks at, or walks to the ACTUAL scene entity while naming it -- "Queso," and the cheese wheel is right there on the counter. Comprehensible input with zero support language. Needs a presentation channel from dialogue to scene (gesture tags resolving to entity references, emote/camera support). Floor version (in-text reference to the entity by name) ships with the repair ladder; this is the ceiling.
- Content-aware modality probes: the teacher SCANS existing world content without ever changing it -- the scene lexicon compiler already ingests item labels, documents, and lore pages, so it can know that the newspaper in the player's inventory talks about shopping, prices, and farming. It then probes comprehension of a concept across modalities, diegetically: reading ("Have you read the paper? What did it say about the price of milk?"); and once lexicon audio and STT land (watchlist), listening and speaking against the same concept. If the player has not read it, nudge rather than quiz: "You should have a look -- then we can talk about the price of milk." Requires a content-to-concept index at compile time (a cheap compiler extension), inventory/read-state awareness for the teacher, and probe phrasing through the normal constraint path. The FSRS item model already carries the concept side; probe outcomes feed observations like any other evidence.
