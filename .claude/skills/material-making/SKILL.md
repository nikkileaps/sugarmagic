# Material making: spec sheet to seamless texture set

You are turning one ChatGPT-generated spec sheet image into five production
texture maps. Everything in this skill was verified in practice on the first
two materials (red_brick and rough_wood, 2026-09-02). The traps below are not
hypothetical; each one shipped a defect or nearly did. A defect you miss here
does not stay hidden - it surfaces in nikki's Blender viewport under real
light, and she is QA.

Hold one sentence above all others while you work: **the five map panels on
the spec sheet are five different paintings of similar walls, not five views
of one texture.** Almost every trap in this pipeline is that sentence,
wearing a different disguise.

## The core truth about spec sheets

This was discovered the hard way, so take it as settled:

- The five map panels are five different paintings of similar walls. Their
  feature layouts disagree. Never assume cross-panel registration. If you
  are ever tempted to believe two panels line up, verify with a
  gradient-magnitude edge overlay - do not trust your eyes on this.
- Panels are roughly quarter-resolution crops of the advertised 2048
  texture, and they are NOT tileable.
- Consequence for what gets harvested: basecolor pixels come from the
  basecolor panel; the normal from the normal panel; roughness from the
  roughness panel. The height and AO panels are NOT harvested at all.
  Height is computed by integrating the composed normal; AO is derived from
  that height. That way normal, height, and AO describe one consistent
  surface instead of three unrelated paintings.
- Cross-map registration happens at the shared layout grid level - brick
  boundaries align because the composed grid is shared - never at pixel
  level. Faces will not match blotch-for-blotch across maps, and that is
  fine: the spec sheet itself has that property.

## Pipeline (proven order)

### 1. Crop the panels

Crop the map panels out of the spec sheet. Measure the panel boxes and inset
a few pixels past the borders so no frame pixels leak in.

### 2. Detect each panel's structure grid

Each panel is a different painting, so each panel needs its own grid
detection - never reuse one panel's grid on another.

What "recessed" looks like differs per map type:

- basecolor: desaturation (mortar and gaps are gray)
- normal: deviation from flat (128, 128, 255)
- roughness: lighter than the surface
- height / ao: darker

Find horizontal bands via row-profile peaks, then per-row vertical joints
via column-profile peaks. `harvest_panels.py` does this and draws debug
overlays. ALWAYS look at the overlay image yourself before harvesting - a
grid that is wrong here poisons everything downstream, and the overlay is
where it is cheapest to see.

### 3. Harvest complete cells only

Every cell edge must sit on a verified structure line (a mortar joint
center).

THE TRAP: a feature that runs off the panel edge looks bounded but is not.
You will look at a brick touching the panel border and think "that brick
looks complete, I can take it." Do not. Cutting a face mid-feature stamps a
raw cut edge into every placement of that cell across the tile. This shipped
a broken wall once - nikki caught fragments of bricks bleeding into other
bricks in the composed output. If the column profile shows no structure
line, there is no joint there, whatever your eyes say. The profile is the
authority; eyes lose this argument.

### 4. Compose a 1024 tile on a seeded layout

Paste with modulo (wrap-around) coordinates so the tile is seamless BY
CONSTRUCTION - seamlessness is never something you fix afterward. Feathered
joints (8-16 px) land in the recess areas.

Grid materials (brick, `compose_wall.py`):

- Running bond layout with an EVEN row count - an odd row count breaks the
  vertical wrap.
- Cells resized to uniform grid cells including their half-mortar margins.
- Avoid picking the same source cell adjacent horizontally or vertically.

Banded materials (wood, `compose_wood.py`):

- Full-width row strips. All segments within a row sample the SAME source
  y-window so the band phase aligns at joints.
- Segment x-windows come from a shuffled partition, not pure random.
- Flips must be uniform per row. Independent per-segment flips put mirror
  images side by side and create chevron artifacts - this happened.
- NO vertical flips at all for banded materials. Alternating the band slope
  direction integrates into a herringbone height weave - this also
  happened.

### 5. Normal map math (exact, not optional)

- Horizontal flip of a normal cell = mirror + invert the red channel.
  Vertical flip = mirror + invert the green channel. A flip without the
  channel inversion is a wrong normal, silently.
- Neutralize each cell's mean X/Y to 128 before pasting. A whole cell
  averages facing straight out; skip this and the wall patchworks with tint
  and the height integration is corrupted.
- Renormalize vectors to unit length after all feathering and resizing.
- CHECK THE PANEL'S RED CHANNEL CONVENTION. On red_brick the spec normal's
  red channel was mirrored relative to OpenGL. The symptom is diagnostic:
  after integration, vertical recesses come out as ridges while horizontal
  ones are correct valleys - which is impossible for a consistent field, so
  when you see it, this is the cause. Fix: invert the red channel of the
  composed normal. The convention varies per material - red_brick needed the
  inversion, rough_wood did not - so run the integration and read the
  symptom fresh for every new material.

### 6. Height and AO (computed, never harvested)

Height = FFT Poisson integration of the composed normal on the periodic
domain. Convention (OpenGL, image rows increasing downward):
`dh/dx = -nx/nz`, `dh/dy_img = +ny/nz`.

High-pass the result to kill the wall-scale drift the integration
accumulates: sigma 60 (of 1024) for brick, sigma 30 for wood, then
percentile-normalize. Wood also gets its contrast compressed (x0.55 around
0.5) because spec wood is nearly flat.

AO = 1 - depth * positive part of (blurred height - height), blur sigma
~8-10, depth ~0.45-0.6.

### 7. Basecolor enhancement (optional; chosen for both shipped materials)

Replicate clarity-upscaler (`philz1337x/clarity-upscaler`), creativity
0.25, resemblance 1.0, scale_factor 2, fixed seed. This is content-locked
enhancement of harvested pixels, not generation - verify drift (mean abs
diff vs a Lanczos-2x of the input) stays small, around 2-4.

Diffusion breaks wrap edges. So run it twice: once on the tile, once on a
half-tile-rolled copy, then splice - the rolled run is un-rolled, and its
interior covers the first run's edges through a feathered distance-to-edge
mask. `enhance_clarity.py` does all of this and fails loud if seams exceed
the baseline.

NEVER run diffusion on a normal map. A normal map is vectors, not colors;
the thought "I'll just run the enhancer on the normal too, it worked on the
basecolor" produces garbage vectors. Real-ESRGAN
(`nightmareai/real-esrgan`) is the safe deterministic fallback when
enhancement is wanted without diffusion risk.

Replicate practicals: token at replicate.com/account/api-tokens; billing
must be set up before any run; it is a US company owned by Cloudflare. The
token goes in a scratchpad file, never the repo. Costs per run: fractions
of a cent (esrgan) to about 10 cents (clarity).

### 8. Verify BEFORE nikki sees it, every round

Every defect that has ever been found in this pipeline (brick fragments,
height speckle, dark-band false gouges, chevron mirrors, herringbone weave,
the red-channel mirror) was visible in an intermediate image that had not
yet shipped. The verification pass is where you catch them instead of her.

- Seam metrics: mean abs diff of opposite edge rows/columns vs an interior
  adjacent-column baseline; the wrap must be at or below the baseline. Do a
  roll-by-half visual check too.
- Proof sheet of all five maps plus a 2x2 tiling of the basecolor.
- Lit preview: lambert-shade the basecolor with the composed normal under a
  raking light (light ~ [-0.45, 0.55, 0.7]); compare against the spec
  sheet's hero render and flat render. This catches relief problems without
  a Blender round trip. `compose_wall.py` has the function.
- Zoom into the actual output pixels looking for defects: fragments,
  speckle, chevrons. Trust numbers and screenshots over assumptions. Fix
  causes at the source; never patch a symptom with blur.

### 9. Ship

Copy the final PNGs to `textures/`, tell nikki what changed in plain terms,
and let her verify in Blender. She is QA; her screenshots come back
annotated with what reads wrong under real light.

## Blender usage (for advising nikki; verified working)

One material across differently sized props: Texture Coordinate (Object
output) -> Mapping (scale 0.83 uniform = one repeat per 1.2 m) -> each Image
Texture node set to Box projection, blend 0.2. Objects need Ctrl+A apply
scale.

Why object space: UV space is per-object 0-1, so an identical Mapping scale
gives a different physical density on every object. Box projection in object
space is therefore the default for environment props.

Box-projected normal maps are an approximation; acceptable at this
stylization. If it bothers her on a hero asset, that asset gets a real
unwrap.

## Iteration discipline (what actually made this succeed)

- Look at every intermediate image yourself before proceeding. Not the
  final output - every intermediate. This is the single habit that caught
  every defect listed above before it shipped.
- When a defect appears, find the mechanism before editing anything. Each
  fix in this skill came from naming the exact cause (a threshold on raw
  luminance, a threshold on smoothed luminance, per-segment flips, and so
  on). A fix applied before the mechanism is named is a symptom patch, and
  symptom patches here take the form of blur - forbidden above for a
  reason.
- Show nikki side-by-side comparisons at decision points (original vs
  candidates, spec panel vs output at matched zoom) and let her pick.
- Do not generate style with image models. gpt-image-1 repainting from
  references drifted twice and was abandoned. Enhancement of harvested
  pixels - content-locked, low creativity - is the only sanctioned model
  use.

## The traps, one more time

These are the moments that have actually bitten. If you remember nothing
else at hour three:

1. Five different paintings, not five views. Harvest basecolor, normal,
   roughness from their own panels; compute height and AO from the composed
   normal; register at the grid level only.
2. A panel-edge feature that looks complete is not complete. No structure
   line in the profile = no joint, whatever your eyes say.
3. Flipped normal cells need their channel inverted; every cell's mean X/Y
   neutralized to 128; vectors renormalized; and the red channel convention
   checked per material.
4. Uniform flips per row for banded materials, and no vertical flips at
   all.
5. Never diffusion on a normal map. Enhancement runs twice with a
   half-roll splice or the wrap seams break.
6. Look at every intermediate before proceeding, and name the mechanism
   before fixing anything.
